The DO staking pool still runs the very code that drained it on Oct 31. The fix is a migration by its admin back to the standard staking code (10920) — the same restore another Garuda pool already received on Oct 31.

Who signsThe Garuda DAO only — the pool's admin

What a revoke is: one MsgRevoke message, signed by the granter, naming the grantee and the exact permission type being taken back. The msg_type_url must be copied exactly from the grant itself (open the granter on the explorer and read it) — a revoke with the wrong type URL revokes nothing. 🔧 How a repair works

When something goes wrong, this is the playbook — in order:

  • ① Detect — this watchtower, the Telegram watch and the hourly tripwire check the token, the pool, the pair, the bad wallets and DO Chain against the verified record. A change alarms with the time it happened.
  • ② Warn & contain — there is no pause button on DO's current contracts, so containment is people: warn holders and stakers at once, tell them not to deposit into the pool or trade into the pair until it is explained, and snapshot balances for the claims ledger.
  • ③ Fix at the source — the fix cards above: the DAO restores the pool code, granters revoke their permissions, and the KuCoin freeze pack goes in with every new transaction hash.
  • ④ Verify — every claimed fix is checked against the chain before anyone calls it done: a restore must come from the DAO's own address, a revoke must show on-chain.
  • ⑤ Resume — only when the contracts verify against the record again does the watch stand down. The owner then accepts the new state into the record — deliberately, by hand.

On DO's current contracts there is no pause and no recall — that gap is exactly why this watch and these fix cards exist. The control room above shows the standard a hardened replacement would ship with.

🔑 Who can press what

  • The contract admins alone. The token's admin is the DO team's wallet; the pool's admin is the Garuda DAO. Only those wallets can migrate or change their contracts — not this page, not a server, not a bot.
  • This page holds no keys. Nothing here can sign, send, or move anything. Every fix ends in somebody's own wallet app, signed by the person who owns the key.
  • Only a granter can revoke their own grant — if a dangerous grant appears, the fix is the granter's one signature. This page's job is to make sure they hear about it fast.
  • Alerts fire when it matters — the watch logs every bad-wallet move with its time and alarms on security-classified moves, so a stolen key cannot finish a dangerous change in silence.
